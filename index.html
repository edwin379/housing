Is the house outgrowing the paycheck?

Every person on this page is the same height: that's an average income. Every house grows or shrinks with its price compared to that income. Scroll, and watch the houses move with the real numbers.

By Edwin · Data: OECD price-to-income ratio (2015 = 100), MLIT Real Estate Price Index (2010 = 100)
How to read it

One person, one house, one year: 2015

In 2015 the OECD sets every country's price-to-income ratio to 100. So in 2015 the house and the person are the same height. A house twice as tall means homes cost twice as much compared to income as they did in that country in 2015.

Question 1

Did homes get less affordable in the last 10 years?

Scroll from 2015 to 2025. Canada's house grows to 131, the US to 126, Australia to 120. Japan grows too, to 114. Only Korea shrinks, to 74.

First answer: yes, in 7 of 8 countries.

Question 2

But what if I go back to 1990?

This surprised me. In 1990, Japan's house was huge: 182. That's the bubble economy. Korea's was 345. Then they shrink for 20 years.

Canada, Australia, New Zealand and the UK start small and keep growing. Their homes roughly doubled compared to income.

The same data as lines

Two worlds moving in opposite directions

Since 1990, Japan's ratio fell 37%. Canada's rose 80%. So the "housing crisis" is real, but mostly in English-speaking countries.

Japan looked fine. Too fine, so I checked inside Japan.

Question 3 · Japan, MLIT data

Which kind of home got expensive?

The Japanese government index splits homes by type, with 2010 = 100. By December 2025, land is 118.3 and detached houses 120.5.

Condos (マンション) are 221.2. They more than doubled.

Where

The condo tower is a city story

In the Tokyo area (南関東), the condo index was 218.1 in September 2025, while detached houses there were 123.2.

Condos near stations are what young city workers usually buy. For them, Japan did get much less affordable.

What I found

The answer changed twice as I kept digging

1 · First chartSince 2015, homes got less affordable in 7 of 8 countries. Looks like a global crisis.
2 · Longer viewSince 1990, Japan (−37%) and Korea (−78%) became more affordable. The crisis is mainly Canada, Australia, NZ, the UK and the US.
3 · Inside JapanThe calm average hides condos: up 121% since 2010, versus about 20% for land and detached houses.

Limits

The OECD ratio compares each country with its own 2015, not countries with each other: a shorter Japanese house does not mean Japan is cheaper than Canada in yen or dollars. The Japan type data are published snapshots (2010 average, November 2020 seasonally adjusted, December 2025 original series); the condo tower moves smoothly between them only for animation.
